Our Royal Story

Our Ethos

Atithi Devo Bhava

"The guest is indeed an incarnation of the Divine. At RajMahal, hospitality is not a profession—it is a sacred royal legacy."

RajMahal Palace stands as a living testament to Rajputana architecture, blending the symmetrical geometry of Mughal gardens with local pink sandstone and intricate hand-carved arches. For nearly three centuries, these walls have hosted grand coronation feasts, deep state negotiations, and international celebrations.

In translating this historical sanctuary into a five-star resort, our guiding star has been absolute architectural preservation. We employed legendary local artisans to carefully clean the original lime murals, restore brass gates, and maintain the ancient brick layouts.

Every single suite features block-print fabrics woven by fifth-generation weaver families, coupled with carefully curated family heirlooms on display.

We believe that true luxury lies in absolute personalization. Your hosts are highly trained ambassadors of the heritage, ensuring your stays are seamless, private, and thoroughly unforgettable.

Through the Centuries

Chronicles of RajMahal

1729

Royal Sanctuary Commissioned

Maharajah Sawai Jai Singh II commissions the estate as a private royal retreat and orchard pavilion for the Queen, featuring symmetrical channels of running river water and grand stone arches.

1821

The British Residency era

Designated as the official high-security residence of the British Political Resident in Rajputana, witnessing major historical conferences, treaty signings, and military assemblies.

1958

Opening to Global Elites

Converted into an ultra-luxury boutique hotel, quickly establishing itself as a global sanctuary for world leaders, monarchs, and artists including Queen Elizabeth II, Jackie Kennedy, and Lord Mountbatten.

2021

Historical Grand Restoration

A magnificent, highly meticulous architectural restoration brings five-star cooling, heating, acoustic insulation, and modern plumbing systems, fully hidden behind historic sandstone structures.

"We do not inherit this palace from our ancestors; we borrow it from our descendants. Our duty is to ensure its grandeur remains clean, pure, and absolutely timeless."

— Sawai Padmanabh Singh of Jaipur
